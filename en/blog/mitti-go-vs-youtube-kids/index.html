How is Mitti-GO different from YouTube Kids?

YouTube Kids picks videos by age and recommendations; in Mitti-GO the parent chooses channels first, and the child watches only them. Here's the difference.

YouTube Kids is already made specifically for children. So why would you need Mitti-GO?

It's a fair question. The difference isn't that one app is "safe" and the other isn't. The difference is, above all, in how content gets chosen.

How does YouTube Kids work?

YouTube Kids is a separate kids' version of YouTube with far fewer videos and channels available.

  • groups
    Age categoryThe parent picks the child's age category.
  • tune
    Video selectionYouTube's systems decide which videos fit that category.
  • bar_chart
    RecommendationsRecommendations can also be shaped by the child's watch and search history.
  • shield
    FilteringGoogle uses automated filtering systems and manually reviews part of the content.

So in the default mode the flow looks roughly like this:

route

YouTube Kids picks suitable content → the child watches it → the parent blocks individual videos or channels if needed.

And how does Mitti-GO work?

With Mitti-GO we decided to go the other way.

  • person
    The parent picks channels firstOnly then does the child get access to videos.
  • check_circle
    A feed from allowed channelsIf a parent allows 5 channels, the app builds the child's feed from those channels' content.
  • block
    No "recommended" channelsA new channel shouldn't just show up in the feed because an algorithm decided to recommend it.
thumb_up

The principle is simple: the parent chooses → Mitti-GO shows → the child watches.

But YouTube Kids also lets you pick content by hand

Yes — and that's worth pointing out.

YouTube Kids has an "Approved content only" mode. In it, parents can manually allow specific videos, channels and collections. Search is turned off for the child in this mode.

smart_display
Approved content onlySearch turned off

So the idea of a parent-made whitelist isn't unique in itself.

The difference is that for Mitti-GO this approach is the foundation of the app, not an extra mode.

What about Shorts?

We believe the decision about short videos should also be the parent's. That's why Shorts are off by default in Mitti-GO.

slow_motion_video
ShortsOff by default

If a family wants short videos, the parent can allow them. Even then, the content still has to come from allowed channels.

family_restroom

Our job isn't to decide for parents what their child can or can't watch. Our job is to give parents the tools to decide it themselves.

More on this in "Why Shorts are off by default in Mitti-GO".

No random channels

This is exactly what we want to make the core principle of Mitti-GO.

A child opens the app and sees a familiar space:

  • their cartoons
  • their educational channels
  • their favorite creators — from a list the parents put together

No need to keep checking what the algorithm will recommend next.
