Should parents watch YouTube with their child?

Yes — especially in the preschool years. Why watching together helps, what kids risk watching alone, what to talk about and how much to watch together.

Yes — especially in the preschool years. Watching together helps a child not just watch but learn: understand what they see, build language and thinking skills. It also makes watching safer.

That doesn't mean sitting next to them every minute. But the first episodes of a new channel, new topics and the bedtime cartoon are best watched together. Here's why — and how to make that time worthwhile.

Why watching together matters

  • psychology
    Your child understands moreYou can explain, ask questions and talk about what they saw. With an adult nearby, a story is easier to follow.
  • forum
    Language growsYou explain new words; your child retells and answers questions.
  • shield
    Fewer risksYou notice right away if a video isn't suitable or makes them anxious.
  • favorite
    A stronger bondWatching together is time for talking, trust and nice moments.
  • star
    Healthy habits take rootYou choose channels together and agree on rules and limits.

What watching together gives your child

  • Builds language and vocabulary
  • Teaches them to ask questions and think
  • Helps tell reality from fiction
  • Shapes a healthy attitude to screen time
  • Makes watching calmer and more mindful

The risks when a child watches alone

On regular YouTube a child can easily stumble on unsuitable content:

  • fast-paced, overstimulating videos
  • scary or aggressive scenes
  • ads and unwanted recommendations
  • content that isn't right for their age
help
Unsuitable content

On regular YouTube it's still possible: the algorithm doesn't know your child's age.

Time is harder to manage

A child can watch longer than they should — one video follows another.

Watching turns into passive time: nobody explains or asks.

A “one more” habit can form

Without guidance, kids ask for more and find it harder to stop.

What to do while you watch

  • forum
    Talk about itAsk: “What do you think just happened?”
  • help
    Answer questionsEven simple questions matter for development.
  • mood
    Watch your child's feelingsIf something is scary or confusing, explain it or switch to something else.
  • pause_circle
    Take pausesStop the video and talk about an interesting moment.
  • favorite
    Set an exampleA calm, mindful attitude to screens helps your child build healthy habits.

What to talk about while watching

Simple questions turn a cartoon into a conversation:

Who is the hero? What are they doing?How are they feeling?Why did they do that?What can we learn from this?What did you like most?

How much to watch together

There are no strict rules, but balance matters. For preschoolers, the WHO recommends no more than one hour of screen time a day.

scheduleAges 2–4up to 1 hour a dayas the WHO recommends
scheduleAges 5–6about 1–1.5 hours a daya guideline — less is better
lightbulb

It's not only about time but also what and how your child watches. More in “How much screen time does a preschooler need”.

How to choose videos and channels

  • Right for their age
  • Teach and develop
  • No scary or aggressive scenes

For a ready-made list, see “10 safe YouTube channels for preschoolers”, and for a quick way to check any channel — “5 simple questions”.

Everyday tips

  1. Choose quality channels — and only those.
  2. Watch short, clear videos, especially with little ones.
  3. Pause and talk — “What's happening?”, “What do you think?”.
  4. Link what you see to real life: spotted it in a cartoon? Find it at home too.
  5. Offer an activity afterwards: a game, a walk or something creative.
  6. Keep an eye on time and agree on it in advance.
