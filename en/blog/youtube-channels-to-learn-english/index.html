Which YouTube channels help kids learn English?

7 channels where English isn't just the voice-over language but part of the learning — and how to make watching actually help.

Children can meet English not only through lessons and flashcards. Songs, short stories, cartoons and simple dialogues let them hear real speech and gradually pick up words in context.

But here's the catch: not every English-language kids' channel actually helps with learning. If a child just watches bright animation and barely engages with the speech, the benefit may be smaller than it seems.

So we've picked channels where English isn't just the voice-over language but part of the learning.

7 channels for learning English

1

Super Simple Songs

One of the clearest options for a first encounter with English. It was created by English teachers in Japan who deliberately made songs slower and simpler for young learners. There are songs about colours, animals, body parts, counting, weather, family and actions.

Good for:first wordsthe alphabetcolours and numbersanimalssimple actions

Don't just play songs in the background — do the moves and say the words together: hear jump, jump; hear clap, clap. Your child links the word to an action, not just a translation.

2

LearnEnglish Kids by British Council

For more structured learning. Songs, short stories, videos and exercises on vocabulary, pronunciation, reading and grammar. Aimed mainly at children aged 5–12.

Good for:kids who know a few wordsvocabularyshort storieslisteningfirst grammar
3

Dream English Kids

Lots of songs and learning videos that repeat words and phrases: ABC, numbers, colours, animals, food, weather, transport, family, clothes. The website has flashcards and class materials.

Your child doesn't just learn the word dog — they hear simple patterns:

It’s a dog.One, two, three…

Short, repeated phrases are especially handy at the start.

4

Maple Leaf Learning

Materials made specifically for teaching English as a second or foreign language (ESL/EFL): songs, phonics, flashcards, games and worksheets. Videos that repeat one pattern several times with a clear picture are especially useful.

What color is it?It’s red.

Your child starts recognising the whole phrase, not just the word red.

5

Fun Kids English

For kids who love music and movement: phonics, the alphabet, simple sentences, verbs, numbers, animals, body parts.

Turn a video into a mini game: play the body-parts song, pause it and ask:

Where is your nose?

Your child points to their nose — then carry on. The screen becomes something you do together.

6

English Singsing

Not just songs but simple dialogues too: Nursery Rhymes, Dialogue, Story, Phonics, Rap and Vocabulary. Great once your child knows single words and is ready for sentences.

What’s your name? — My name is…How are you? — I’m fine.What are you doing?
7

Steve and Maggie

English through mini stories, play and a live host — for children starting English as a foreign language. A good step from learning songs to more natural speech: not only “Blue. Red. Green.” but real phrases.

Where is it?Look!Come here!What is this?

How to make watching actually useful

Watch together

Sometimes watch with your child and talk about what they saw.

Repeat words

Ask your child to repeat simple words and phrases from the video.

Use it in real life

Bring new words into everyday situations.

Pick the right level

Start with simple songs and move on to dialogues and stories.

Watching cartoons alone doesn't guarantee your child will start speaking English. But don't turn a cartoon into an exam — just repeat words now and then and use familiar phrases in everyday life. After a video about colours, animals or food:

Give me the red car.Where is the dog?Do you like bananas?

Bit by bit, English stops being just a sound coming out of the phone.

What about regular YouTube?

Even if you've found a great learning channel, after a video YouTube may suggest something completely different. So it matters not only what your child watches, but where they can go next.

It's not about the number of videos

There's no need to turn every free hour into a language lesson. It's far more useful to regularly watch clear videos at the right level, repeat a few words and then use them in real life.
