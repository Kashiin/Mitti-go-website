How to tell if your child spends too much time on the phone

Screen time isn't always harmful in itself. What matters is noticing changes in your child's behaviour and habits — here's what to look for.

Screen time isn't always harmful in itself. What matters more is how your child's behaviour and habits change.

8 signs of too much screen time

bedtime
Sleep problems

Struggles to fall asleep, asks to "watch a bit more", tired in the morning.

bolt
Irritability

Gets upset when it's time to turn off a cartoon or hand back the phone.

toys
Losing interest in other things

Used to love playing, drawing and going out — now less and less.

center_focus_strong
Trouble focusing

Gets distracted faster and listens less, especially on longer tasks.

sentiment_dissatisfied
Mood swings

Cries, gets angry or withdraws when the phone isn't available.

visibility
Health complaints

Complains about eyes or headaches more often, leans in close to the screen.

forum
Less social

Avoids family and friends, prefers the screen to real play.

restaurant
Mealtime trouble

Asks for the phone at the table or only eats with cartoons on.

lightbulb

One sign alone is no reason to worry. Pay attention if you see several at once and they last for weeks. If eyesight, sleep or headaches concern you, talk to your paediatrician.

!

What can it lead to?

Lower attention
Sleep problems
Less movement
Harder to manage emotions
check

What can you do?

Agree on rules in advance

Explain how much cartoon time there is each day.

Set clear limits

Pick a specific time for watching and stick to it.

Offer alternatives

Playing together, walks, books, crafts.

Lead by example

Kids follow rules more easily when they see adults do the same.

It's all about balance

A phone can be useful and fun when used mindfully. Mitti GO helps parents create a calm, safe space for watching.
