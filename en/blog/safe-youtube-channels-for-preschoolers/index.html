10 safe YouTube channels for preschoolers

Trusted channels that build language and teach counting and emotions — from Super Simple Songs to Numberblocks. Plus how much to watch and how to add them to Mitti-GO.

YouTube is a huge library of kids' videos. But next to good cartoons it's easy to stumble on pointless, overly loud or simply unsuitable clips — and the recommendation algorithm has no idea your child is four.

We've picked 10 channels trusted by millions of parents over the years. They build language, teach counting and understanding emotions — and stay calm and easy to follow for preschoolers.

How we chose the channels

  • Only official channels of studios and educational organisations
  • A clear age range — from 1 to 6
  • Calm pace, simple language and easy-to-follow stories
  • Real value: language, numbers, emotions, social skills, curiosity
  • No scary scenes, rudeness or grown-up jokes

10 channels for preschoolers

CoComelon – Nursery Rhymes1–5 years

Popular songs, nursery rhymes and simple learning stories about baby JJ and his family. The animation is bright, so short sessions work best.

  • builds language and listening
  • teaches basics: colours, counting, daily routines
  • simple, clear animation
Sesame Street2–6 years

The legendary educational show with Elmo, Cookie Monster and Big Bird, made by the non-profit Sesame Workshop.

  • social skills
  • emotions and friendship
  • letters, numbers, logic
Super Simple Songs1–5 years

Simple, catchy songs in English, created by English teachers especially for the youngest learners.

  • language development
  • new words and phrases
  • calm pace and simple animation
Numberblocks3–6 years

Cheerful BBC stories where numbers are the characters. They make counting and early maths easy to grasp.

  • learning numbers
  • logic and counting
  • visual examples
Peppa Pig – Official Channel2–6 years

Simple, kind stories about family, friends and everyday life. Episodes are short — about five minutes.

  • relatable everyday situations
  • language development
  • short episodes
Blippi – Educational Videos2–6 years

Explore the world with Blippi: vehicles, jobs, animals and more — in real places, not just in animation.

  • real objects and situations
  • broadens horizons
  • great for curious kids
PBS KIDS2–6 years

The official channel of America's public broadcaster: educational cartoons and shows for little ones, such as Daniel Tiger.

  • builds independence
  • helps understand emotions
  • a safe, trusted environment
Masha and the Bear2–6 years

Kind, funny stories about friendship and the adventures of mischievous Masha. The cartoon comes in many languages.

  • teaches kindness and helping others
  • sparks imagination
  • clear, short episodes
Hey Duggee2–6 years

Adventures at the Squirrel Club from the BBC: the animals earn badges for new skills and important life lessons.

  • teamwork
  • respect and kindness
  • simple, clear lessons
Khan Academy Kids2–6 years

The educational channel of the non-profit Khan Academy: reading, counting and first steps in learning.

  • reading, maths, logic
  • thinking skills
  • calm, high-quality content
info

Ages are a rough guide. Watch a couple of episodes yourself — you know your child best. For a quick way to check any new channel, see “5 simple questions: should you allow this channel?”.

How much is enough

Even the best channel can't replace play, time outdoors and talking together. The World Health Organization recommends:

scheduleAges 2–5up to 1 hour a dayand less is better

More in “How much screen time does a preschooler need”.

How to make watching worthwhile

groups
Watch together

At least the first few times: you'll see whether the channel fits and can talk about what you watched.

forum
Talk and ask questions

What did you like? What did the character do? That turns a video into a conversation, not background noise.

music_note
Repeat songs and words

Sing along and copy the moves — especially with English-language channels.

tv_off
Don't leave it on in the background

Background video gets in the way of play and focus. Put it on to watch, switch it off when you're done.

How to add these channels to Mitti-GO

  1. Open the parent area from the Profile tab — with your PIN or Face ID.
  2. Manage Content → add a channel. Find it by name, @handle or link — or pick it from Mitti GO's suggestions.
  3. Allow the whole channel or only the playlists you want — for example, just the English songs.
  4. Done. Your child sees only the channels you added, and Shorts are off by default.
